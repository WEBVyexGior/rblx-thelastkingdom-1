# The Last Kingdom — Story (Canon)

**Status:** Canon locked — Book One
**Version:** 1.0 (living document)
**Developer:** Cookies11f

> This document locks the **narrative canon** of *The Last Kingdom*. It expands the story
> vision in `GameDesignDocument.md` (§1 Game Vision, §3 Story Overview, §4 Campaign
> Structure) into the fixed facts that all code, content, missions, and dialogue must
> respect. The **GDD remains the absolute source of truth**; where this document adds detail
> the GDD does not yet contain, that detail is marked **[PROPOSED]** and becomes canon only
> once confirmed.

> ⚠️ **Names are provisional.** *Asterfall*, and every NPC / place / faction name in this
> document, are **working names** — kept changeable on purpose. What is locked is the
> **structure** (the 40-year return, the fall, the curse, the Fallen King, the TLK2 seed),
> **not** the labels. Do not treat any proper name here as final.

---

## 1. Logline

*Forty years ago the finest warriors of Asterfall marched north and vanished. They return to
find their kingdom dead, the dead walking, and their own king the source of the curse.*

---

## 2. Where this game sits in the saga

- **The Last Kingdom** (this game) is **Book One** of a planned saga.
- Internally it plays out as **five chapters** (GDD §4 Campaign Structure). Book One's climax —
  its final boss — is **The Fallen King of Asterfall** (internal Chapter 5).
- The brief's phrase *"Fallen King as the final boss of Chapter 1"* is read here as **the final
  boss of the first installment (Book One)** — this whole game — to avoid colliding with the
  internal chapter numbering. **[Confirm this reading.]**
- Book One tells a **complete, self-contained story** — the player finishes a journey, not a
  cliff-hanger (GDD §4) — while deliberately **seeding *The Last Kingdom 2*** (see §11).

---

## 3. The World Before — Asterfall at its height

Forty years ago the **Kingdom of Asterfall** was one of the strongest kingdoms in the region
(GDD §3.1) — [PROPOSED] a walled realm of stone keeps and farmland, its great castle raised
over older, deeper foundations. It stood for order in a hard land.

Then a **mysterious threat appeared beyond the northern borders** (GDD §3.1). Its nature was
never fully known — [PROPOSED] only that it came from the far north, and that the king judged
it grave enough to commit his very best against it.

---

## 4. The Expedition — why the warriors left

To meet the northern threat, **the king sent his finest warriors on a long expedition**
(GDD §3.1). The player characters *are* these warriors — [PROPOSED] a sworn company expected
to be gone for months.

They were never meant to be gone for forty years.

---

## 5. The Silence — the fall of the kingdom

The expedition **disappeared**. Months became years; the world changed without them (GDD §3.1).
While they were away — or because of what they marched against — **Asterfall fell**:

- the kingdom collapsed,
- villages and homes were destroyed,
- the people they left behind were lost,
- **the dead began to walk.**

What the returning warriors believed was home is now a **graveyard** (GDD §3.1). The exact
sequence of the fall is uncovered *by the player* through exploration and lore — it is the
central mystery, not exposition handed out up front (GDD Pillar 2 — Story First).

---

## 6. The Origin of the Curse

The truth beneath the mystery (GDD §3.3):

- Facing the ruin of his kingdom, **the king reached for an ancient power hidden beneath the
  castle.**
- **That power was never meant for human hands.**
- Instead of saving Asterfall, it **corrupted** it — and corrupted **him**.
- The king became the **source of the undead curse** that now walks the land.

[PROPOSED] The power beneath the castle predates Asterfall; the castle was raised over it.
Whether it is connected to the original northern threat is **left open on purpose** (§11, §12).

---

## 7. The Return — the player's entry point

The game opens as the **survivors of the expedition finally come home** (GDD §4, Chapter 1 —
"The Return Home"). They arrive expecting a kingdom and find ruin. The opening hours are about
**discovery and dawning horror**, not combat: arrival, first exploration, meeting the few
survivors, and the realization that Asterfall has fallen.

---

## 8. The Antagonist — The Fallen King

- The final enemy is **not a random monster** (GDD §3.3). He is **the last ruler of Asterfall**,
  consumed by the power he invoked.
- The final battle is **not only** about defeating an enemy — it is about **freeing the king
  from the corruption** that consumed him (GDD §3.3). The climax is **tragic**, not merely
  triumphant.
- [PROPOSED] Freeing the Fallen King **lifts or breaks** the curse over Asterfall, enabling the
  **kingdom-restoration** beat of Chapter 5 (GDD §4).

---

## 9. The Player's Goal

**Restore Asterfall and discover the source of the undead curse** (GDD §3.2), across:

1. reaching the lost kingdom,
2. discovering what happened during the forty years,
3. helping surviving villagers,
4. recovering lost knowledge,
5. entering the fallen castle,
6. confronting the source of the corruption.

---

## 10. Campaign spine (narrative connective tissue)

Mirrors GDD §4 Campaign Structure and adds the *story throughline* between chapters. Detailed
per-scene scripting is later content work.

| Chapter | Story beat | Question the player answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — The Return Home | Arrival; the kingdom is gone; first survivors | "What happened here?" |
| 2 — The Lost Villages | The fall retold through ruins, documents, rescues | "How did it fall?" |
| 3 — The Road to Asterfall | The land turns hostile; the curse's reach grows | "How deep does this go?" |
| 4 — The Fallen Kingdom | Into the castle; the truth of the king and the power | "Who did this — and why?" |
| 5 — The Fallen King | Confrontation; freeing the king; restoration | "Can it be undone?" |

---

## 11. Seeds for *The Last Kingdom 2* [PROPOSED]

Book One resolves Asterfall's curse but leaves the **larger thread open**:

- The **northern threat** that sent the warriors away forty years ago was **never resolved** —
  the expedition vanished before finishing what it set out to do.
- The **ancient power beneath the castle** has an **origin** the king only borrowed from; the
  curse of Asterfall may be **one symptom of something older and wider**.

> Design intent: Book One must feel **complete on its own** (GDD §4). These seeds stay quiet —
> a final discovery, a line of lore, an unanswered question — never a cliff-hanger that cheats
> the player of an ending.

---

## 12. Canon locks (must be respected by all content & code)

**Hard canon (structure) — from the GDD, do not contradict.** The proper names below are
**working names** (see the note at the top of this doc): the *structure* is locked, the
*labels* are not.

- The kingdom (working name: **Asterfall**).
- The warriors (the player characters) return after **forty years** away.
- They were sent **north** by the king against a **mysterious threat**, and **vanished**.
- Asterfall has **fallen**; **the dead walk**.
- The curse's source is the **king**, who invoked an **ancient power beneath the castle** that
  was **not meant for humans** and **corrupted** him.
- The final boss is **The Fallen King of Asterfall**; the climax **frees** him from corruption.
- The player's goal is to **restore Asterfall and uncover the source of the curse**.

**[PROPOSED] — canon only once you confirm:**

- Names for: the warrior company, the ancient power, key survivor NPCs, the northern threat.
- Whether the northern threat and the buried power are the **same** force (or linked).
- The exact mechanism of "restoration" after the king is freed.

## 13. Open story questions (lock before Chapter content is built)

1. Do the **player characters have named identities / backstories**, or are they blank-slate
   returning warriors?
2. How much of the fall is **shown vs. discovered** — and in what order across Chapters 1–4?
3. What is the **emotional through-line** of the co-op fantasy (brothers-in-arms returning
   together)?
4. Tone of the **ending**: bittersweet restoration, hopeful rebuild, or ambiguous?
5. How do the **northern threat** and the **TLK2 seed** surface in Book One without derailing it?
