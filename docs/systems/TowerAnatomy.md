# What makes a tower read as real

Notes behind `tools/BuildBattleground.luau`. A cylinder with square teeth on top
reads as a chess piece. These are the parts that make it read as a building
somebody defended.

Bottom to top:

| Part | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Batter (talus)** | The splayed, sloping thickening at the foot | Deflects dropped stones outward and resists undermining. It is the single most recognisable feature of a real tower and almost always the thing missing from a fake one. |
| **Plinth roll** | A projecting rounded band where the batter meets the shaft | Sheds water off the slope. Gives the eye a base line. |
| **String courses** | Horizontal bands up the shaft | Mark the floors inside. Without them a tower is a pipe. |
| **Putlog holes** | Small square holes in a rising spiral | Where the scaffolding poles sat while it was built. Nobody filled them in. Free authenticity. |
| **Cross loops** | Arrow slits, splayed wider on the inside | The archer needs to swing; the attacker needs a target he cannot hit. The cross bar is for a crossbow. |
| **Lancet windows** | Narrow, arch-headed, high up | Only the upper floors get real windows. Ground floor windows are how a tower falls. |
| **Garderobe chute** | A projecting box, part way up, with a hole | The latrine. Ugly, universal, and instantly convincing. |
| **Corbel table** | A ring of stone brackets near the top | Carries the overhang above it. |
| **Machicolations** | Boxes projecting out on those corbels, with gaps between | You drop things through the gaps onto whoever is at the wall foot. THE mark of a serious tower. |
| **Parapet & merlons** | The battlement wall on the overhang | Merlons often have their own slits. |
| **Allure** | The walkway behind the parapet | Somewhere to stand. Visible through the gaps. |
| **Bartizans** | Small turrets corbelled out near the top | Cover the tower's own blind spots. |
| **Stair turret** | A slimmer drum attached to one side, rising higher | The spiral stair has to go somewhere, and it always overshoots the roof. |
| **Conical roof + finial** | Timber cone, iron spike, banner | Reads at distance. The banner is what dies first. |
| **Iron anchor plates** | Small crosses on the shaft | Tie rods holding the floors in. Tiny detail, enormous payoff. |

## Ruin

A ruined tower is not a shorter tower. What survives is what was thickest:

- The **batter always survives**; the parapet almost never does.
- Breaks are **diagonal**, not level — masonry fails along a line.
- **Floor sockets** are left in the inner face where the beams pulled out.
- The **stair turret often outlives the tower it served**, because it is a
  narrower, stronger tube.
- Rubble sits in a **cone** at the foot, on the side it fell.
